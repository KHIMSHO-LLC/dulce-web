## What this calculator does

Type the average glucose from your sensor report and it gives you the **GMI** (glucose management indicator), with the formulas published by Bergenstal and colleagues in 2018:

- GMI (%) = 3.31 + 0.02392 × average glucose in mg/dL
- GMI (mmol/mol) = 12.71 + 4.70587 × average glucose in mmol/L

You can enter the average in mg/dL or mmol/L. An average of 154 mg/dL (8.6 mmol/L), for example, gives a GMI of 7.0% (53 mmol/mol). Next to it you'll see the A1C estimated with the older ADAG formula, which comes from a different study and gives a slightly different number.

## GMI isn't your lab A1C

GMI is a calculation from sensor readings; A1C is a blood test. GMI used to be called "estimated A1C" and was renamed because so many people saw their lab result differ from the number in their app. A gap between them is normal: anemia, pregnancy, how long red blood cells live and some hemoglobin variants all move A1C without changing your average glucose. To go from A1C to average glucose, use the <PageLink to="/tools/a1c-calculator">A1C calculator</PageLink>, and our <ArticleLink to="hba1c">A1C guide</ArticleLink> explains the test.

## How much data GMI needs

The formula was built from sensor data, and the time in range consensus recommends at least 14 days with the sensor worn about 70% of the time. An average from two or three days gives a GMI that can change a lot from one week to the next.

## GMI in Dulce

The Trends tab in Dulce shows your average glucose and the estimated A1C from GMI for 7, 14, 30 or 90 days, worked out from your sensor's readings. The <ArticleLink to="trends">Trends guide</ArticleLink> explains it. GMI is information for you and your care team, not a basis for changing treatment on your own.
