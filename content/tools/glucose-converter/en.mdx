## How to convert mg/dL to mmol/L

Divide the mg/dL value by **18**. To go the other way, multiply mmol/L by 18.

- 100 mg/dL ÷ 18 = **5.6 mmol/L**
- 7.0 mmol/L × 18 = **126 mg/dL**

The exact factor is 18.016, which comes from the molar mass of glucose (180.16 g/mol). Meters, sensors and the Dulce app round it to 18, so this converter uses 18 too and the numbers match what you see every day. At normal glucose levels the difference is never more than 0.1 mmol/L.

<Callout type="warning">
This converter is for **glucose only**. Cholesterol (total, LDL, HDL) and triglycerides use different factors: cholesterol mg/dL ÷ 38.67 = mmol/L, triglycerides mg/dL ÷ 88.57 = mmol/L. Using the glucose factor on a cholesterol result gives a wrong number.
</Callout>

## Why there are two units

It depends on where you live. The United States, Spain, most of Latin America, Germany, France and Italy use **mg/dL**. The UK, Ireland, Canada, Australia and the Nordic countries use **mmol/L**. If you read a British forum or study, you will almost certainly see mmol/L.

## Common values

| mg/dL | mmol/L | What it usually means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 54 | 3.0 | Level 2 hypoglycemia (act now below this) |
| 70 | 3.9 | Bottom of the target range |
| 100 | 5.6 | Upper limit of normal fasting glucose (without diabetes, below this) |
| 140 | 7.8 | |
| 180 | 10.0 | Top of the target range |
| 250 | 13.9 | Above this: level 2 hyperglycemia |
| 300 | 16.7 | |

The 70 and 180 mg/dL (3.9 and 10.0 mmol/L) limits come from the <ArticleLink to="time-in-range">international consensus on time in range</ArticleLink>. Your care team may set different targets for you.
