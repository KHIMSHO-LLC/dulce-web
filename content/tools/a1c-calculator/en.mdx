## What this calculator does

It has two modes:

- **I have my A1C.** It works out the **estimated average glucose (eAG)** that matches that A1C, using the formula from the ADAG study: eAG (mg/dL) = 28.7 × A1C − 46.7. It also gives your A1C in mmol/mol, the IFCC unit many European labs report.
- **I have my sensor's average glucose.** It works out the **GMI** (glucose management indicator), the number sensor reports show: GMI (%) = 3.31 + 0.02392 × average glucose in mg/dL. Next to it you'll see the A1C estimated with the ADAG formula, so you can see they don't always agree.

## GMI and A1C are not the same thing

A1C is a blood test: it measures how much hemoglobin has bound to glucose over roughly three months. GMI is a calculation from your sensor readings. It was renamed (it used to be called "estimated A1C") precisely because so many people found their lab result didn't match the number in their app.

It is normal for them to differ. How long your red blood cells live, anemia, pregnancy or some hemoglobin variants all move A1C without your average glucose changing. That's why the two numbers are read together, not one instead of the other.
