Exercise is one of the moments when glucose moves the most, and one of the moments when it's hardest to pull out a phone. If you train with a Garmin, the **Dulce data field** puts your glucose on the activity screen next to pace, power or heart rate. It can also **save your glucose into the activity**, so after the workout you see the glucose line in Garmin Connect alongside everything else.

This guide covers what the data field shows, how to add it, how saving works and what to keep in mind. The watch app and watch face are on the <PageLink to="/garmin">Dulce for Garmin page</PageLink>.

## What the data field shows

The field adapts to the space you give it on the activity screen:

- **Large** (a full screen or most of it): a label, your number large with the trend arrow, the change, the reading's age and a short graph of the last hour.
- **Medium**: the number with the arrow, the change and the age.
- **Small**: the number and the arrow.

Numbers are coloured by zone: red below 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L), green from 70 to 180 (3.9 to 10.0), orange above. On Edge bike computers in day mode the field uses darker colours so it reads well on a light background.

## What you need

- **Dulce Pro** (14-day free trial), or following someone who has it. See <PageLink to="/pro">what Pro includes</PageLink>.
- **Dulce connected to your sensor** through LibreLinkUp, Dexcom Share, Medtronic CareLink or Nightscout, on **the latest version of Dulce**.
- **Garmin Connect** on your phone, running in the background, and the phone with you. On a run that means carrying the phone; on a bike it can sit in a pocket or bag.
- A Garmin watch or **Edge bike computer** with Connect IQ 3.1 or later. The full list is on the <PageLink to="/garmin">Garmin page</PageLink>.

## Add the data field

<Steps>
<Step title="Install the data field">
In the Connect IQ Store, find **Dulce** and install the data field. Garmin Connect syncs it to your watch or Edge.
</Step>
<Step title="Put it on an activity screen">
On the watch, open the activity you use (Run, Bike, Hike…), go to its settings, then **Data Screens**, pick a screen and a field, and choose the Dulce field from the Connect IQ fields. On an Edge you can do the same from the activity profile.
</Step>
<Step title="Link it">
Start the activity. The field shows a 6-character code. In Dulce on your iPhone, go to **Settings › Garmin watch**, type the code and tap **Link watch**. If the code is gone, restart the activity to get a new one.
</Step>
<Step title="Train">
The field now shows your glucose during every activity that has it on screen.
</Step>
</Steps>

## The glucose line in Garmin Connect

With **Save glucose in the activity** turned on (it is on by default), the data field writes your glucose into the activity file while you record. Garmin calls this a developer field: it travels with the activity, so after you save it, the activity in Garmin Connect shows a **glucose chart** for the workout and your **average glucose** for the session.

A few details:

- The unit saved is the one you use in Dulce when the activity starts, mg/dL or mmol/L.
- Only activities with the Dulce field on one of their screens record glucose.
- To turn saving off, open the data field's settings in the Connect IQ section of Garmin Connect and switch off **Save glucose in the activity**.

That line is useful afterwards: how your glucose moved over a long ride, whether it dropped at the same point in the last three runs, what it did after an interval session. Talk through patterns with your care team; the chart describes, it doesn't tell you what to change.

## How fresh is the number?

A data field can only fetch new data in the background, and Garmin allows that **every 5 minutes**. Your sensor might produce a reading every minute (FreeStyle Libre) or every 5 minutes (Dexcom, Medtronic), but the field updates at most every 5 minutes. The field shows the reading's age, and an old reading turns grey. During exercise glucose can change quickly, so treat the age as part of the reading.

## Limits worth knowing

- **No alerts from the field.** Dulce's low and high alerts still reach your phone as notifications, and Garmin can show phone notifications on the watch. Your sensor's own alarms keep working.
- **The phone has to come along.** Without it, the field can't get new readings and the age keeps counting.
- Want to see your glucose outside workouts too? The <ArticleLink to="garmin-watch-face">Dulce watch face</ArticleLink> keeps it on your wrist all day.

<Callout type="warning">
Exercise can lower or raise glucose quickly, and a reading on the watch can be up to 5 minutes old. Don't base treatment decisions on the watch: if you feel low or the number surprises you, stop, check with your sensor or a fingerstick, and follow the plan you agreed with your care team for exercise. Dulce is not a medical device.
</Callout>
