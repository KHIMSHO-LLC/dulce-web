A continuous glucose monitor can make life with diabetes easier at any age, and the American Diabetes Association now recommends one for older adults with type 1 diabetes and for those with type 2 on insulin. There's no single best CGM for older adults. The right one depends on eyesight, dexterity, the phone they use and who helps them. This guide is for older adults and for the sons and daughters looking out for them.

## What to look for in a CGM

Ask the care team which sensors are available and covered, because that differs by country and insurer. Then compare them on what matters day to day:

- **Applying it.** Can they put on the sensor alone, or will someone help?
- **Alarms they can hear.** Try the volume and the sound with them.
- **Phone or reader.** A phone makes sharing possible. A standalone reader stays private to the person holding it.
- **Who answers.** Decide who responds to a low at night before it happens.

## Why less tapping matters

Many older people don't want to learn an app, and they don't have to. With Dulce the reading comes to them:

## Goals can be different

Consensus targets for older people or those at higher risk of lows are gentler than the usual ones: more than 50% of the time between 70 and 180 mg/dL, less than 1% below 70, and less than 10% above 250. The American Diabetes Association also says goals should be individual. Set the range their care team gives you, not one from the internet.

## What Dulce can't do

- A phone must stay near the sensor and online, because that's how readings leave it. If they leave their phone at home, you'll see the last reading's time fall behind.
- Readers don't send readings to the internet, so Dulce can't show them.
- The Live Activity and family following are Dulce Pro features for the person being followed, with a 14-day free trial. Followers pay nothing.
