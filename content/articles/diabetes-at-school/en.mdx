Sending a child with type 1 diabetes to school means trusting other adults with something you've been watching around the clock. A written plan and a glucose sensor you can follow from work make that much easier. This guide is for parents: what to agree with the school, what to do about phones in class, and how to follow your child's glucose during the day without calling every hour.

## Agree a plan before the first day

International guidance for schools says a diabetes plan should be developed and agreed with parents before the child starts. Each country has its own forms and rights. In the US it's usually a Diabetes Medical Management Plan, often with a 504 plan. In Europe, ask your diabetes team and your national association what your school has to provide. Whatever the form, the plan should cover:

- How to recognise and treat a low, and where the supplies are.
- Who at school is trained, and who covers when they're away.
- Meals, snacks, sport and exams.
- What the sensor alarm means and who responds to it.
- Who to call, and in what order.

## The phone problem

Sensors usually send readings to a phone, so a school phone ban is the first thing to solve. The phone reading the sensor has to stay close to your child and online, or nobody can see the readings. The American Diabetes Association's school guidance says the plan should set out how the CGM is managed at school and that the school should follow it. Ask for the phone to be written into the plan as a medical need.

For younger children, ask where the phone will be and who hears it. It may be kept by the teacher, as long as it stays within range of the sensor.

## Following from work

If the Libre app on your child's phone shares with your LibreLinkUp account, you can open Dulce on your own iPhone with that account. Nothing changes on their side. Teens with their own iPhone can use Dulce and invite you from Settings → Followers, as the <ArticleLink to="child-glucose">guide to seeing your child's glucose</ArticleLink> explains.

You'll then see their glucose on your own Lock Screen and get your own low and high alerts. The second parent and a grandparent can follow as well, there's no limit, and followers pay nothing. Inviting followers is a Dulce Pro feature with a 14-day free trial.

## Keep calm in the school day

- **Agree who acts.** If every follower reacts to the same low, the school gets three calls.
- **Wait for the trend.** A sensor lags, and an arrow often tells you whether a low is already being treated. The <ArticleLink to="alerts">alerts guide</ArticleLink> explains the settings.
- **Mind the teenager.** Short messages like "Are you OK?" answered with one tap beat a call in the middle of class, and they can be turned off.
- **Set alerts for the school day.** Too many false alarms teach everyone to ignore them.

If you've only just received the diagnosis, start with the <ArticleLink to="newly-diagnosed">guide to the first weeks</ArticleLink>.

## What to give the teacher

One page: a photo of your child, what a low looks like for them, what to do, and your numbers. Teachers don't need an app or a login. School staff aren't expected to follow your child's data on their own phones unless the plan says so.

<Callout type="warning">
Dulce is not a medical device and doesn't replace the sensor's alarms, which should stay on near your child. If the last reading is old, you're seeing the past. The school follows the plan agreed with your diabetes team, not an app.
</Callout>
