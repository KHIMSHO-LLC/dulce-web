**The short answer:** a Signal Loss alarm means your FreeStyle Libre sensor hasn't talked to the Libre app on your phone for **20 minutes**. Move the phone within **10 metres (33 ft)** of a Libre 3 or 3 Plus sensor, wait **2 to 4 minutes**, and if it doesn't come back, turn Bluetooth off and on, then restart the phone. While the signal is lost, your glucose alarms don't work, so check with a fingerstick if you need a number.

This guide is about the phone that reads the sensor. If the sensor phone is fine but a family member sees nothing in LibreLinkUp, read <ArticleLink to="librelinkup-not-working">LibreLinkUp not working</ArticleLink> instead.

## What the Signal Loss alarm means

The Libre 3 and 3 Plus send a reading to your phone every minute over Bluetooth. Abbott's app raises the Signal Loss alarm when the sensor hasn't communicated with it for 20 minutes. It exists to tell you one thing: **your glucose alarms aren't available right now.** Abbott is clear that low and high alarms only reach you when they're turned on and the sensor is within range of the phone.

## The usual causes

According to Abbott, there are three:

- **The phone is too far away.** The range is 10 metres (33 ft) unobstructed for the Libre 3 and 3 Plus, and 6 metres (20 ft) for the Libre 2. Walls, water and your own body shorten it, which is why it often happens at night with the phone on the other side of the bed or charging in another room.
- **Bluetooth is off** on the phone.
- **A problem with the sensor** itself, such as a sensor error.

Two more causes come from how iPhones handle apps in the background:

- **You force-closed the Libre app.** Abbott's quick-fix steps say to make sure you haven't. When you swipe an app away, iOS stops waking it, and it can no longer listen for the sensor.
- **The app lost its Bluetooth permission.** On iPhone, check **Settings → [your Libre app] → Bluetooth** is on, and that notifications are allowed for it so alarms can reach you.

## How to fix it, in order

<Steps>
<Step title="Bring the phone close and wait">
Put the phone within 10 metres of the sensor with nothing in between and wait 2 to 4 minutes. Abbott says that's how long reconnecting can take.
</Step>
<Step title="Open the Libre app">
Open it once so it's running, and tap the "i" symbol next to the message if it's there for more details. Don't swipe it away afterwards.
</Step>
<Step title="Turn Bluetooth off and on">
In Control Center or Settings, turn Bluetooth off, wait a few seconds and turn it back on.
</Step>
<Step title="Restart the phone">
If that doesn't work, turn the phone off and on again.
</Step>
<Step title="Contact Abbott">
If the alarm keeps coming back, call Abbott's customer service for your country. It may be the sensor, and replacing it is up to them.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="warning">
While the signal is lost you won't hear low or high alarms. If you have symptoms or need a number to treat, check with a fingerstick meter and follow your care team's plan.
</Callout>

## Are the missing readings lost?

With the **Libre 3 and 3 Plus**, no: Abbott says the app fills in the gap automatically when the sensor is back in range, without scanning. With the **Libre 2**, you scan the sensor to recover the readings it stored, which covers the last 8 hours.

Followers in LibreLinkUp see the gap fill in a little later, once the phone uploads the recovered readings.

## How to make it happen less

- Keep the phone **on the same side of your body** as the sensor at night, not on a charger across the room.
- **Never force-close** the Libre app, and leave Bluetooth on.
- Keep the Libre app's **alarms on** and its notifications allowed.
- If it happens at the same time every night, look at where the phone is then: it's almost always distance or your body between sensor and phone.

## Where Dulce fits in

Dulce doesn't talk to the sensor, so it can't fix a lost signal: it shows what Abbott's app uploads through LibreLinkUp. What it does is make a gap obvious. Instead of showing an old number as if it were current, the home screen says **"Last reading 25 min ago"**, the reading is marked as stale, and the Apple Watch, widgets and Lock Screen show its age too. Family members who follow you in Dulce see **"Old data"** with the minutes, so nobody mistakes a frozen number for a live one.
