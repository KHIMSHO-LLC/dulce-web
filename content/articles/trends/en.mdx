The number right now tells you what to do right now. Trends tell you what's really going on: whether nights are the problem, whether you spike after meals, or whether this month is better than last. That's what the **Trends** tab in Dulce is for.

## What Trends shows

At the top you pick the period: **7, 14, 30 or 90 days**. Below it are three cards:

- **Time in range**: the percentage of readings inside your target range, split into low, in range and high.
- **Avg glucose** for the period.
- **Est. A1C**, calculated **from GMI** (the glucose management indicator) using your average glucose.

Each card is compared with the previous period, so you can see at a glance whether things are getting better or worse.

<Callout>
Why doesn't "Est. A1C" match your lab result? Because GMI is calculated from the sensor and A1C is a blood test. We explain it in detail in our <PageLink to="/tools/a1c-calculator">A1C and GMI calculator</PageLink>.
</Callout>

## Breakdown by day and by time of day

- **Daily breakdown** (or **weekly breakdown** for the longer periods): each bar is a day with its time in range. Tap a day to see its full curve.
- **Time of day**: your time in range split into four parts of the day, night, morning, afternoon and evening. It's the most useful view for finding patterns, like early-morning lows or after-lunch spikes. If nights are your weak spot, read our guide to <ArticleLink to="night-lows">nocturnal hypoglycemia</ArticleLink>.

## How to get the most out of it

1. **Start with 14 days.** The international consensus considers at least 14 days of data necessary for these numbers to mean something. With 7 days, one odd weekend changes everything.
2. **Look for the worst time of day, not the worst day.** A bad day happens. A time slot that repeats (every night, every afternoon) is a pattern worth taking to your next appointment.
3. **Check time below range** before time above. The general goal is less than 4% of the time below 70 mg/dL. We cover it in <ArticleLink to="time-in-range">time in range vs. A1C</ArticleLink>.
4. **Match it with what you log.** If you log carbs and insulin in the <ArticleLink to="logbook">Dulce Logbook</ArticleLink>, it's much easier to understand why a time slot goes wrong.

## Set the range to your targets

By default Dulce uses a 70 to 180 mg/dL range and a 70% time-in-range goal, the consensus targets for most adults. If your care team gave you different ones, change them in **Settings → Target range** and **Settings → Time-in-range goal**: the colors on Home and Trends follow.

If you also use Apple Health, Dulce can show insulin and carb data from other apps next to your sensor data: see the <ArticleLink to="apple-health">Apple Health guide</ArticleLink>.
