Vets now often put a human glucose sensor on diabetic dogs and cats, most often a FreeStyle Libre. It is off-label: the sensor was made and approved for people. Studies show it is clinically useful in both species, with one important catch: it reads too high when glucose is very low. This guide covers what to know before the first sensor, and how to see your pet's glucose on your iPhone and Lock Screen with Dulce.

## How a sensor on a pet works

The vet or a vet nurse places the sensor, in dogs usually on the side of the chest just behind the shoulder blade. From then on it measures glucose all the time, just like on a person, for up to 14 days if it stays on.

The readings go to a phone running the sensor's own app, over Bluetooth. That is the part people forget: **the phone that reads the sensor has to stay near your pet**. Many owners keep a spare phone at home next to the dog bed or the cat's favourite spot for exactly that reason.

## What the studies say

- **Useful overall.** A 2023 veterinary review calls FreeStyle Libre the sensor used most in dogs and cats, with adequate clinical accuracy in both, and lower accuracy in the low range.
- **Lows read too high.** In cats, Libre 2 and Libre 3 overestimated blood glucose in marked hypoglycaemia (below about 55-60 mg/dL). The sensor may say your cat is fine when it is lower than that. **Always check a low reading with a blood glucose meter** before you act on it.
- **Skin and sticking.** In one study of 34 dogs, 71% of sensors stayed on for the full 14 days and 18% caused mild skin redness or crusting. Ask your vet how to protect the site.

## Seeing your pet's glucose with Dulce

Dulce reads the sensor's sharing service, so it works the same way for a pet as for a person: with FreeStyle Libre you share from the sensor app to a LibreLinkUp account and sign in to Dulce with it. The <ArticleLink to="librelinkup-not-working">LibreLinkUp guide</ArticleLink> helps if the share doesn't show up.

Then open **Settings → Targets & units → Who is Dulce for?** and choose **A pet**. Pet mode changes what a person's numbers would get wrong:

- **Your vet's target range.** The range can go much wider than for a person (low limit 40 to 200 mg/dL, high limit up to 500).
- **Line colours.** You choose where the chart line and the number turn red and orange, instead of the human 70 and 180.
- **Critical low level.** You set the level, from 40 to 90 mg/dL, at which the critical alert rings, even on silent if you allow it.
- **Alerts from 40 mg/dL.** Low and high alerts follow the limits your vet gives you.

<Callout type="info">
Pet mode is in Dulce 1.0.7 and later. Dulce never suggests a range: it starts from standard values and expects you to enter the ones from your vet.
</Callout>

## What Dulce can't do

- Dulce doesn't tell you how much insulin to give or when to feed. Your vet decides that.
- If the phone near your pet is out of range or offline, the reading stops updating and Dulce shows how old it is.
- Pet mode, alerts and the logbook are free. The Live Activity, Apple Watch app and family following are Dulce Pro, with a 14-day free trial.

<Callout type="warning">
Dulce is not a medical device or a veterinary product, and glucose sensors are not approved for animals. If your pet is weak, shaking or collapses, treat it as an emergency and call your vet.
</Callout>
