Your sensor measures glucose by itself, but it doesn't know what you ate or how much insulin you took. Without that, a mid-afternoon spike is just a line going up. Logging is what turns the chart into something you can understand, which is why it has to be quick: if it's a chore, it doesn't happen.

## What you can log

From the **Logbook** tab, or with the **Carbs** and **Insulin** buttons on the home screen, you can add:

- **Carbs**, in grams, with an optional note on what you ate.
- **Insulin**, in units, marked as bolus or basal.
- **Exercise**, in minutes.
- **Manual glucose**, for example a fingerstick reading to check your sensor.
- A free-text **Note**.

## Log it in seconds

<Steps>
<Step title="Tap + or a quick button">
In the Logbook tap **+**, or use Carbs or Insulin on the home screen.
</Step>
<Step title="Pick the amount">
Slide the ruler to the amount or tap one of the shortcuts to add to it.
</Step>
<Step title="Say when">
Now, 15 minutes ago, 30, 1 hour ago or another time. So it doesn't matter if you log it late.
</Step>
<Step title="Save">
Tap **Save**. The entry shows up instantly in your Logbook and as a marker on the chart.
</Step>
</Steps>

Made a mistake? Tap any entry to edit or delete it.

## Everything on one timeline

The Logbook puts what you log and your sensor readings in order, day by day, with the period's time in range, average and GMI at the top. You can look at today or the last 7, 14 or 30 days.

If you use other apps that save insulin or carbs to Apple Health (a smart pen or a food app, for example), Dulce can read them and show them here too, marked with where they came from. See the <ArticleLink to="apple-health">Apple Health guide</ArticleLink>.

## What it's good for in practice

- **Understanding a spike.** Did you go up after lunch because the bolus was late, or because there were more carbs than you counted?
- **Bringing data to appointments.** Your care team understands a week with meals and insulin far better than a curve with no context.
- **Spotting patterns.** Together with the time-of-day view in the <ArticleLink to="trends">Trends tab</ArticleLink>, it's easy to see which meal or time keeps repeating.

<Callout type="warning">
Dulce is a logbook, not a dose calculator: it doesn't recommend how much insulin to take. That decision is yours and your care team's.
</Callout>

## Quick questions

**Do I need Dulce Pro for the Logbook?** No. The Logbook and the Trends tab are in the free version. You only need Pro to read data from Apple Health.

**Can I keep bolus and basal apart?** Yes. When you log insulin you choose bolus or basal, and it shows that way in the Logbook.

**What if I use mmol/L?** Manual glucose is entered in whichever unit you chose in Settings, mg/dL or mmol/L.
