A type 1 diagnosis arrives with a lot at once: injections, carbs, alarms and new words, often while you're still in shock. It can happen at any age, so this guide is for adults who've just been told and for parents of a newly diagnosed child. It doesn't replace what your care team teaches you. It's a map of the first weeks, and of where a glucose sensor and an app like Dulce fit in.

## The first days

Your care team leads this part. Before you leave them, try to be sure you know:

- How to recognise and treat a low, and who around you should know how too.
- What to do when you're ill, and when to test for ketones.
- Who to call, day and night.
- Your own target range. Don't copy someone else's.

## Words you'll hear

| Word | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Hypo | Glucose below 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L) |
| Hyper | Glucose above your target range |
| CGM | Continuous glucose monitor: a sensor on your skin |
| Trend arrow | Where your glucose is heading |
| Time in range | Share of the day inside your target |
| HbA1c | A lab average of about 3 months |
| Honeymoon | A period after starting insulin when your body still makes some of its own |

## Your first sensor

The American Diabetes Association recommends offering a CGM at or soon after diagnosis to children and adolescents who can use it safely, and supports CGM from the onset of diabetes for adults on insulin. Ask your team which sensors are available to you.

In the first days a sensor can feel like too much information. A few things help:

- **One number doesn't mean much.** Look at the trend and the day, not one spike.
- **Set alerts with your team.** Too many at the start only wear you out.
- **Sensors lag behind finger pricks**, especially when glucose moves fast. If a reading doesn't match how you feel, check as your team showed you.

## Give yourself time

Nobody learns this in a week. Numbers will go up and down even when you do everything right, and that isn't failure. Ask for a diabetes educator, and for psychological support if you need it: it's part of care, not an extra.

<Callout type="warning">
Dulce is not a medical device. It doesn't calculate or suggest insulin doses, and it doesn't replace your sensor's alarms or your care team. If you feel unwell or a reading looks wrong, follow the plan they gave you.
