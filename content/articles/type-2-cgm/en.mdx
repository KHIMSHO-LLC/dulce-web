A glucose sensor isn't only for type 1. The American Diabetes Association now recommends considering CGM for people with type 2 on insulin, on other medicines that can cause lows, and on any treatment where it helps. This guide explains what a sensor shows if you have type 2, with or without insulin, what it doesn't do, and how to see the numbers without having to open an app.

## What a sensor shows in type 2

A finger prick gives you one moment. A sensor shows what happens between them:

- How a meal moves your glucose, and how long it takes to come back.
- What a walk after dinner does.
- Whether glucose rises overnight or in the early morning.
- Lows you might not feel, if your treatment can cause them.

## With or without insulin

**On insulin.** This is where the evidence and coverage are strongest. Sensor alerts for lows matter most here, and the <ArticleLink to="alerts">alerts guide</ArticleLink> shows how to set them.

**Without insulin.** Many people use a sensor for a while to learn from it. Coverage varies by country and insurer and is often limited, so ask your care team what applies to you. Some sensors are also sold without a prescription in some countries. Check the list of supported sensors before you buy one.

## Reading the numbers

Time in range is often more useful than a single reading. For most adults the consensus target is more than 70% of the time between 70 and 180 mg/dL (3.9 and 10 mmol/L), but your care team may set another. The <ArticleLink to="time-in-range">time in range guide</ArticleLink> explains it, and the <PageLink to="/tools/a1c-calculator">A1C calculator</PageLink> relates your average to your lab result. Don't change medicine because of what a sensor shows without talking to your doctor.

Dulce shows readings from FreeStyle Libre, Dexcom and Medtronic sensors and from your own Nightscout site. It doesn't ask what type of diabetes you have. Most of what's useful in type 2 is free:

- Live glucose, the trend arrow and the chart.

## Other types of diabetes

If your type 2 diagnosis has never quite fitted, ask whether you've been tested for antibodies: some adults first diagnosed with type 2 turn out to have <ArticleLink to="lada-diabetes">LADA</ArticleLink>. Dulce works the same for gestational diabetes, MODY and other types as long as the sensor is supported, but in pregnancy use only the range your care team gives you.

<Callout type="warning">
Dulce is not a medical device. It doesn't give diet or dosing advice and doesn't replace your sensor's alarms or your care team. Confirm any unexpected reading as they showed you before acting on it.
</Callout>
