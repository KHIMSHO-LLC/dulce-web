If you wear a FreeStyle Libre and an iPhone, you have more options than Abbott's own apps for seeing your glucose at a glance. Sweet Dreams, Gluroo and Dulce all read the same LibreLinkUp data and put it on your Lock Screen, widgets or Apple Watch, but they are built for different people. This guide compares them honestly, using what each one lists on its App Store page today, and explains how to switch without touching your sensor.

</Callout>

## Where the data comes from

All of these apps start from the same place. Your sensor sends readings to the Libre app on your iPhone (FreeStyle LibreLink or FreeStyle Libre 3), that app uploads them to Abbott, and Abbott shares them with **LibreLinkUp**, the account meant for followers. A third-party app signs in to LibreLinkUp and shows those readings in more places. That's why switching between these apps never requires changing anything on the sensor phone.

## Where each one is strongest

**Sweet Dreams** supports far more sensors than anyone else here: Dexcom, Medtronic pumps through CareLink, Eversense, Nightscout and more. Its listing also says its critical alerts can sound when the iPhone is on silent, and it shows readings on CarPlay. If you use a Medtronic pump or several kinds of sensors in one family, it's the stronger choice today.

**Gluroo** is built around logging and talking to your family: food, insulin and notes in a shared timeline, with your glucose alongside. If your household coordinates care in one chat, that's its focus.

**Dulce** is built for people with a FreeStyle Libre who want their number visible without opening an app, in their own language. The whole app is in Spanish, English, German, French and Italian, not just English. The widgets, the logbook for carbs and insulin, and Trends (time in range, average glucose and GMI) are free. Family members follow you for free, and Pro adds the Lock Screen Live Activity, Dynamic Island, the Apple Watch app and alerts checked every minute. With iOS 26 the Live Activity also shows on your car's CarPlay screen. **LibreLinkUp** itself is free and enough if a family member only needs notifications. It doesn't put the number on the Lock Screen, in widgets or on the watch.

## How to switch to Dulce in a minute

If you already use Sweet Dreams, Gluroo or any app that reads LibreLinkUp, you already have everything Dulce needs.

<Steps>
<Step title="Install Dulce">
Download Dulce from the App Store on the iPhone where you want to see the readings.
</Step>
<Step title="Sign in with the same LibreLinkUp account">
Use the LibreLinkUp email and password your current app uses. Nothing changes on the phone that reads the sensor.
</Step>
<Step title="Set your alerts and units">
Choose mg/dL or mmol/L and your low and high limits. Check the <ArticleLink to="alerts">alerts guide</ArticleLink> so you hear a low at night.
</Step>
<Step title="Add your widgets and watch">
Add the widgets from your Home Screen and, with Pro, turn on the Live Activity and the Apple Watch app.
</Step>
</Steps>

If you ever want to go back, your LibreLinkUp account is untouched, so your previous app keeps working with it.

## Which one should you pick?

- You wear a **FreeStyle Libre** and want it on your Lock Screen and watch, in **Spanish, German, French or Italian**: Dulce.
- You want a **free logbook** next to your glucose and a family that follows for free: Dulce.
- You use a **Medtronic pump**, or need alarms that ring on silent: Sweet Dreams.
- Your family mostly needs a **shared log and chat**: Gluroo.

Whatever you choose, keep your sensor app's own alarms on. None of these apps is a medical device or replaces your sensor's alarms.
