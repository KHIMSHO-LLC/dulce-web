Dulce can warn you when your glucose goes low, high or changes fast. Everything is in <Path>Settings › Alerts</Path>. Alerts are free. This page explains what each setting does, then gives a setup many people use at night.

<Callout type="warning">
Dulce's alerts are an extra. Keep your sensor app's own alarms on: they don't depend on the internet the way Dulce does.
</Callout>

## Your alert limits

At the top of the Alerts page, drag the handles to set your **low** and **high** limits, or tap a handle to type a value. The defaults are 70 and 180 mg/dL (3.9 and 10.0 mmol/L). The same limits show as dashed lines on your charts.

Below them you can turn the **low** and **high** alerts on or off one by one.

## Critical low

**Critical low** alerts you at 55 mg/dL (3.0 mmol/L) and below. It always comes through, even during quiet hours or while alerts are snoozed, and it repeats every 5 minutes while you stay that low. You can turn it off, but we don't recommend it.

**Ring on silent and Do Not Disturb** lets that one alert play a sound even when your iPhone is muted. iOS asks you once to **Allow Critical Alerts** for Dulce; if you said no, turn it on in the iPhone **Settings › Dulce › Notifications**.

## Early warnings

- **Rapid change**: tells you when glucose rises or falls fast, before it crosses a limit.
- **Predictive low**: warns you when you're heading low soon. It's off until you turn it on, and it only suggests checking your sensor.

## When and how they sound

- **Low alert sound** and **High alert sound**: tap one to hear it. Very low alerts always use **Alarm**.
- **Alert frequency**: how often the same alert can repeat if nothing changes.
- **Quiet hours**: mutes everything except critical lows during the hours you choose, for example at night.
- **Snooze alerts**: pauses alerts for 30 minutes, 1 hour or 2 hours. Severe lows still come through.

## Two common setups

<Option title="Day and night the same" pick>

Low and high on, critical low on, **Ring on silent** on, no quiet hours.

<Pros>
- You never miss anything.
</Pros>

<Cons>
- Highs can wake you up at night.
</Cons>

</Option>

<Option title="Quieter nights">

Same as above, plus **quiet hours** for your sleep time. Highs and normal lows stay silent during those hours; critical lows still wake you, with sound, even on silent.

<Pros>
- Better sleep, and the dangerous low still gets through.
</Pros>

<Cons>
- A normal low during the night won't sound: only critical lows will.
</Cons>

</Option>

## On your watch and for family

When your iPhone is locked and you're wearing your Apple Watch, iOS sends alerts to your wrist. People who follow you can get their own alerts on their iPhone: see <HelpLink to="family">sharing with family</HelpLink>.
